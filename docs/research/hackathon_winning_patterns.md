# What Wins LauzHack-style Multi-Sponsor Hackathons, and How to Position One Project Across Tracks

Research date: 2026-11-21, which is LauzHack 2026 day 1. Primary LauzHack evidence comes from the official per-year project pages (2023/2024/2025.lauzhack.com/projects), the official rules PDF, and EPFL news. I downloaded those pages and parsed them locally, so the counts and prize labels below are as listed there.

## 1. Past LauzHack winners (main ranking and sponsor challenges), recurring sponsors, and what Logitech/AXA/IMC/Nexthink winners built

### Takeaway
The overall podium at LauzHack is consistently won by working, user-facing AI/CV prototypes with a clear human problem: visually impaired assistance (2023), meeting agents (2024), and Linux support for Logitech devices (2025). In 2025, webcam/CV, gesture and accessibility projects won at least five separate prizes. Logitech challenge winners use Logitech hardware or SDKs (Loupedeck, MX Creative Console, MX haptics, webcams) in a novel workflow. AXA winners are AI tools that protect or assist an everyday person. I found no past IMC or Nexthink challenge winners; those sponsors do not appear in the 2023-2025 challenge lists.

### Inferences
- The strongest LauzHack-specific signal for a webcam/CV accessibility project: in 2025 alone, webcam, gesture, eye-tracking or visually-impaired projects won the Organizers' prize (Birdy), Logitech 1st (MX Sense), Logitech 3rd (JARVIS), JetBrains 1st (gesture and eye-tracking IDE plugin) and Huawei 1st (PawPilot). In 2023, they took 1st overall (SightSync) and 2nd overall (VirtuWheel). This theme is proven, but it is crowded. Differentiation will matter: real hardware integration, a concrete persona, and a robust demo.
- Logitech winners consistently do one of two things: (a) make Logitech hardware do something new (Loupedeck, MX Creative Console on Linux, MX haptics for blind users, Logitech webcam or peripherals in VR/3D), or (b) frame a business case for Logitech, as MX Sense's "market segment" line does. The 2025 1st-overall project was literally a Logitech-ecosystem project, so a strong sponsor-hardware hack can also win overall.
- AXA winners are consumer-protection or "assist a person in a stressful situation" AI tools (lease disputes, conflicts, image Q&A for risk). An accessibility or safety angle, such as risk prevention, health or ergonomics, could plausibly map to AXA.
- Solo and two-person teams win often (Birdy, MX Sense, VirtuWheel, 3DX, SightSync), so team size is not decisive. Execution and focus are.
- One team placed in consecutive years (Podcastify AI, 3rd in 2024; KnowTube, 2nd in 2025, with overlapping members). Both were polished, LLM-based learning tools, which suggests that a clean product framing for everyday users scores well with the main jury.

### Gaps
- I found no published IMC or Nexthink challenge winners. They are absent from the 2023-2025 challenge filters, so 2026 may be their first year as challenge setters, or they may sponsor without a challenge. Their 2026 challenge briefs were not on the public site.
- Prize amounts and 2026 challenge briefs were not published on lauzhack.com.
- I did not get the 2022 and earlier winner lists. The 2022 projects page is nearly empty, and Devpost galleries returned HTTP 403.
- I found nothing on Logitech's judging rubric beyond the winners' descriptions. A LinkedIn post mentioning "first place in Logitech's challenge at EPFL's LauzHack" with the MX Master 4 SDK and haptics surfaced in a search snippet, but I could not verify its year or content.

## 2. LauzHack rules: multiple challenges, team size, judging process

### Takeaway
Yes. LauzHack explicitly allows a team to work on "one or more" sponsor challenges, and the rules state that projects are eligible for multiple prizes ("top overall and top of a challenge"). The 2025 winner lists show one project collecting up to three prizes. Teams are at most 4 people. Judging is Sunday 12:30-15:00. Teams present to an organizer-selected jury and to sponsors, and each sponsor awards at least one prize per challenge. Teams must state explicitly what is novel compared with any existing code they reuse.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 29: "participants must submit and present their project at the end." — [LauzHack Rules PDF](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)
- Rule 30: participants may use existing code or images, but "their work must be novel compared to the resources they use, and they must make the novelty explicit during judging." — [LauzHack Rules PDF](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)
- Rule 31: "Participants present to a jury selected by organizers, and to sponsors." — [LauzHack Rules PDF](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)
- Rules 32-33: organizers award top-project prizes and optionally prizes like "organizers' favorite". Sponsors award prizes for their favorite projects, "at least one per sponsor challenge." — [LauzHack Rules PDF](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)
- Rule 34: "Participants are eligible for multiple prizes, such as top overall and top of a challenge." — [LauzHack Rules PDF](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)
- Rule 35: decisions can be disputed only with evidence of misconduct. Rule 37: rules may be updated up to 7 days before the event. — [LauzHack Rules PDF](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)
- FAQ: "you can choose to work on one or more of the challenges our sponsors will propose. There are prizes for the best hacks overall and the best challenge entries." Teams are up to 4 people, and groups cannot change after the application form is submitted. — [lauzhack.com](https://lauzhack.com)
- 2026 schedule: hacking runs Sat 12:00 to Sun 12:00 (24h nominal, about 20h of effective build after sleep and meals). Judging is 12:30-15:00 and the closing ceremony 15:00-17:00. — [lauzhack.com](https://lauzhack.com)
- Multi-prize examples: logilinux took 1st overall, Logitech Special Mention and JetBrains 4th (2025). showcAIse took 3rd overall and Huawei 2nd (2025). "It's not about winning" took 3rd overall and the AXA winner (2023). MakeGreatBox took the Organizers' prize and Bobst runner-up (2024). — [2025](https://2025.lauzhack.com/projects), [2023](https://2023.lauzhack.com/projects), [2024](https://2024.lauzhack.com/projects)
- 2019: a 24h build, with the top 3 judged on technical excellence, novelty and functionality — [EPFL news](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/augmented-reading-experience-takes-top-prize-at--2)

### Inferences
- With roughly 2.5h of judging for about 70 projects, jury and sponsor visits are likely short and science-fair style, in line with MLH norms of 2-5 minutes per team (see section 4). Have a 2-minute and a 30-second version of the pitch ready, plus a sponsor-specific 60-second version.
- Because sponsors judge their own challenge separately, the team should prepare one tailored "why this is a [Sponsor] project" line and demo moment per targeted challenge, rather than one generic pitch.
- Rule 30 means the team should explicitly say what they built versus what they reused, for example "MediaPipe for landmarks; we built the gesture grammar, haptic mapping and X." That also answers the "technical depth" question.

### Gaps
- The public rules do not describe a pre-jury/final-jury structure, a cap on the number of challenges, or a submission platform (Devpost or otherwise) for 2026. Check the opening ceremony and Discord.
- I found no published judging rubric weights for LauzHack.

## 3. Accessibility / tech-for-good webcam and CV winners: what wins, and pitfalls

### Takeaway
Assistive CV projects win frequently, at LauzHack and elsewhere, because they produce an immediate, visible "wow" (a webcam reacts to the body) and a sympathetic persona. Experienced critics warn about "disability dongles": projects built without input from disabled people, sign-language gloves being the classic case. Judges and the disability community reward evidence of user grounding and penalize one-way, gimmicky "translation" claims.

### Cited Findings
- LauzHack accessibility and CV winners: SightSync (2023 1st overall), PawPilot (2025 Huawei 1st), MX Sense (2025 Logitech winner, haptics for visually impaired users), Birdy (2025 Organizers' prize, webcam posture and gestures), JetBrains gesture and eye-tracking plugin (2025 JetBrains winner), VirtuWheel (2023 2nd, hand pose) — [2023](https://2023.lauzhack.com/projects), [2025](https://2025.lauzhack.com/projects)
- 2019 winner Augmented Reading built its live demo on a webcam plus physical outputs (speaker, RGB bulb) — [EPFL news](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/augmented-reading-experience-takes-top-prize-at--2)
- Elsewhere: Ghana DI-Hack 2025's top prize went to EchoSign, a real-time sign-language translation tool. A 2026 MCP & AI Agents hackathon track winner was a webcam vision agent with an ASL mode — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/01/16/di-hack-techs-hackathon-ghana/), [Solo.io blog](https://www.solo.io/blog/celebrating-the-winners-of-the-2026-hackathon-for-mcp-ai-agents)
- "Disability dongle" (coined by Liz Jackson) describes well-intended accessibility inventions, often student or hackathon projects, made without meaningful disabled input. They get praise and media attention while being impractical or redundant. Sign-language gloves are the canonical example — [Disability dongle glossary](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/glossary/69aae6a221c0aad631a2dba5), [Selfdefined](https://deploy-preview-30--selfdefined.netlify.com/definitions/disability-dongle/)
- A Cornell study found that overly positive feedback leads to poor assistive tech. Gloves capture only fingerspelling, a small part of the ASL lexicon, and miss facial expressions and larger gestures. The deaf community has largely rejected them — [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/322973)
- SignAloud (UW sign-language gloves) won the Lemelson-MIT Student Prize but was criticized as a one-way bridge "not necessarily one the deaf community has been clamoring for" — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2016/05/17/478244421/these-gloves-offer-a-modern-twist-on-sign-language)
- A Devpost judge (NEAR) flagged "solutions searching for a problem" as a key failure. Another judge (Atlassian) asks, "Is that finished product something that I would want to use?" — [Devpost judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)

### Inferences
- To pre-empt "did you talk to users?", name a specific persona and condition (for example, motor impairment after RSI, ALS, or temporary injury), cite a real statistic or user quote, and be honest. "We validated with X people at the event" or "next step: test with [organization]" is better than overclaiming.
- Prefer framing as control and input augmentation (hands-free or low-effort computer control with Logitech hardware) over "translation for the deaf", which attracts the dongle critique.
- Demo risks for webcam CV: lighting in the judging room, background people triggering detection, latency, camera permissions, and a laptop that cannot also drive a projector. Mitigate with a calibration step, a confidence threshold, a visible "detected gesture" overlay so judges see the system working, and a backup video.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable, specific TreeHacks or HackMIT accessibility grand-prize write-ups. My searches did not surface official winner pages for them.
- I found no first-hand sources quoting judges asking "did you talk to users?" at these events. The pitfall is inferred from the dongle literature and general judge advice.

## 4. Judging patterns: demo vs slides, technical depth, story, wow moment, polish, stacking tracks

### Takeaway
Across judge write-ups, a working live demo that shows the user experience within about 90 seconds dominates. Next come story, problem understanding, and meeting each challenge's stated requirements. Judges punish one-dimensional projects, template reuse, ambiguity, and ideas visibly retro-fitted to a track. Swiss peer events (HackZurich, START Hack) add feasibility, business potential and UX as explicit criteria.

### Cited Findings
- Square judge: "sometimes people pick only one of the judging criteria but miss everything else". Ideas that look built for another purpose and then forced to fit the contest read poorly — [Devpost judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Databricks judge: "Presentation and storytelling matters and it can be a huge part of your success". "Ambiguity is a red flag" — [Devpost judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Google judge: winners "depart the most from that template". Judges try the project first, and videos are the backup. Use the provided sponsor resources — [Devpost judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- Several judges: many submissions fail basic requirements, so check each one — [Devpost judging tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)
- JetBrains judging-table notes (via search summary, since the page returned 403): a simpler project with a clear, confident demo beats a complex one with a confusing demo. Walk judges through the user experience. Show something working within about 90 seconds. Remove every point where the demo could stall (pre-fill forms, mock slow API calls). Rehearse out loud and time it — [JetBrains blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/)
- Suggested pitch timing: about 15 seconds for the hook, 60-90 seconds of demo, about 30 seconds of tech, within 2-3 minutes total — [search summary of judge guides](https://dev.to/cloudinary/how-to-win-a-hackathon-1377) (secondary)
- MLH's recommended science-fair judging gives about 3-5 minutes per team, or 2 minutes of presentation and demo plus 1 minute of Q&A. Judging takes about 2-3 hours overall — [MLH Judging Plan](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan)
- HackZurich 2022 used main-jury and sponsor-jury assessment on Devpost (creativity, viability, UI/UX, technical strength). The top 25 advanced to finals with 2-minute pitches. In 2017-2018, teams chose which sponsor prize to be considered for — [HackZurich 2022 Devpost](https://hackzurich-2022.devpost.com/), [HackZurich 2018](https://hackzurich18.devpost.com/) (via search summary, since the pages returned 403)
- START Hack 2018 Devpost criteria included innovativeness, business potential and UI/UX (20% each). Finalists pitch to a jury that includes unicorn founders, for a CHF 10,000 grand prize (2025) — [START Hack 2018 Devpost](https://kelaenderkoe.pages.dev/patterns-https-start-hack-2018-6129.devpost.com), [START Hack 2025](https://lu.ma/fxkr17xp) (secondary)
- LauzHack 2019 jury criteria: technical excellence, novelty, functionality — [EPFL news](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/augmented-reading-experience-takes-top-prize-at--2)

### Inferences
- On stacking tracks: LauzHack's rules are permissive, and multi-prize wins happen. The winning pattern is one coherent core product whose single feature naturally touches each sponsor (logilinux was a Logitech project that also had developer-tool value for JetBrains). A project with N bolted-on integrations is not the pattern. Given the "forced fit" warning, limit targets to the 2-3 challenges where a sponsor-specific feature can be shown live, and tailor the opening line to each sponsor judge.
- The "wow moment" in LauzHack winners is physical or real-time: lights and sound reacting to reading, haptics guiding a blind user, a gesture driving an IDE. Put it in the first 30 seconds of the demo.

### Gaps
- There is no LauzHack-specific evidence of how judges react to teams claiming many tracks. The guidance above is inferred from general judge commentary.
- I found no quantified weighting of demo vs slides at LauzHack.

## 5. Practical 20-hour build tactics for 3-4 people

### Takeaway
Scope to one end-to-end "golden path" that works by mid-build. Freeze features well before the deadline, then spend the remaining time on robustness, a backup video and pitch rehearsal. Assign explicit roles, including a dedicated pitch and demo owner.

### Cited Findings
- Remove stall points, mock slow calls, and rehearse with people outside the team, ideally people like the judges — [JetBrains blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/06/how-to-win-a-hackathon-notes-from-the-judging-table/) (via search summary)
- Have each demoer do dry runs so they "understand how short 2 minutes really is". Show the product rather than describing it — [MLH: How to judge a hackathon](https://news.mlh.io/how-to-judge-a-hackathon-finding-the-chosen-one-05-15-2014) (via search summary)
- Demo videos: start with the elevator pitch and explain quickly how the app addresses the hackathon — [Devpost demo video tips](https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video)
- LauzHack's own framing: no need to be a programming master, only to "glue together existing libraries and interfaces to create your demo" — [lauzhack.com](https://lauzhack.com)
- 2026 timeline: hacking Sat 12:00-Sun 12:00, dinner 18:30, midnight snack, breakfast 7:00-9:00, judging from 12:30 — [lauzhack.com](https://lauzhack.com)

### Inferences (suggested plan, keyed to the 2026 schedule)
- **Sat 12:00-13:30, scope lock.** Pick a persona and a 3-step golden path. Map one visible feature to each targeted sponsor. Talk to sponsor booths and mentors to confirm what they value; Logitech staff are on site and judge.
- **Sat 13:30-18:30, skeleton end-to-end.** Get the webcam pipeline, the core interaction and the output (hardware, haptics, UI) wired with hard-coded parts. Milestone: an ugly but complete loop before dinner.
- **Sat 19:30-02:00, depth.** Build the real model or logic and the sponsor-specific integrations. Record a first backup video of a working run as soon as the loop works.
- **Sun 02:00-07:00, sleep in shifts.** The pitch owner drafts the deck and story during this window.
- **Sun 07:00-09:30, feature freeze around 09:00.** After that, fix bugs only, add UI polish and the on-screen debug overlay, and handle lighting and calibration.
- **Sun 09:30-12:00.** Record the final backup video, submit, and rehearse the 2-minute, 30-second and per-sponsor versions at least three times each. Prepare the Rule 30 "what we built vs reused" slide.
- **Roles for 3-4 people:** (1) CV/ML lead, (2) integration and hardware/SDK lead (Logitech), (3) frontend/UX and demo-reliability lead, (4) pitch, story, user research, video and sponsor liaison, who also tests the demo cold.
- **Fallbacks:** backup video on a local file (not streaming), a pre-recorded input clip so the CV pipeline can run on recorded footage if the room lighting fails, a second laptop with the same build, and a phone hotspot.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative source specifying the optimal feature-freeze time or milestone plan. The plan above is a synthesis, not a sourced fact.
- I found no LauzHack-specific statement on whether a pre-recorded video is accepted in place of a live demo. Rule 29 requires teams to "submit and present".
