# Prior-Art Landscape: Webcam/Microphone AI for Accessibility, Learning and Productivity (LauzHack 2026)

Scope note: research done 2026-11-21, with about 20 web searches and fetches. Many product claims come from vendors or listicles, and those are marked. Items I could not verify are listed under Gaps and are not presented as facts.

## Sign language: recognition/translation products, camera-feedback learning apps, Swiss context, community attitudes

### Takeaway
The big players have moved in. Google DeepMind shipped on-device ASL-to-English sign-to-text (SL2T) in Gboard and Live Transcribe on Pixel 11 in August 2026. Sorenson has bought Hand Talk and OmniBridge. Nvidia and Georgia Tech/Google run free camera-feedback ASL learning tools. A generic "sign language translator" hackathon project would therefore duplicate well-funded work. It would also likely annoy Deaf communities, who are openly skeptical of translation hype. What remains open is narrower: Swiss sign languages (DSGS/LSF-CH/LIS), and especially LSF-CH in Lausanne, which has almost no camera-feedback tools. Also open: non-manual features (face, mouth, head), short Deaf-led learning experiences built for memory, and tools that put the effort on hearing people rather than Deaf users.

### Cited Findings
**Big-tech translation**
- Google DeepMind, 12 Aug 2026: the SL2T (sign-language-to-text) model powers sign-to-text dictation in Gboard and Live Transcribe, starting with ASL to English, with Pixel 11 the first device. It is free. Training data is over 100,000 hours across 50+ sign languages, about a quarter of it ASL. On-device MediaPipe Holistic extracts pose landmarks, so raw video does not go to the server, and the model translates landmarks directly to text with no glosses. It scores 70 BLEURT zero-shot on FLEURS-ASL. The team also worked on left-handed and one-handed signing and on hallucination when no one is signing. Governance is through an AI Sign Language Advisory Committee (AISLAC) of Deaf organisations — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/putting-sign-language-ai-into-users-hands/)
- SignGemma, announced at Google I/O 2025 as an open sign-to-text model: developer-forum threads show researchers still unable to get access after the expected date — [Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/how-to-get-access-to-signgemma/106766), [forum 2](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/signgemma-availability/111432). One aggregator, updated Aug 2026, says the open weights are still pending behind an interest form, with ASL-English the only strong pair. This is unverified by a primary source — [aixploria](https://www.aixploria.com/en/?p=38909)
- MultiLingual reported that a broader SignGemma release was expected in Q4 2025 — [MultiLingual](https://multilingual.com/google-signgemma-on-device-asl-translation/)

**Commercial translation companies**
- Sorenson acquired Hand Talk and OmniBridge (Nimdzi dates this to January 2025). The stated aim is to speed up AI sign-language translation, and OmniBridge does real-time two-way ASL/English — [Daily Moth](https://www.dailymoth.com/blog/sorenson-acquires-two-companies-on-ai-driven-automated-sign-language-translation), [Nimdzi 2025](https://www.nimdzi.com/nimdzi-language-technology-radar-report-2025/)
- Hand Talk is still marketed in 2026 as a text/voice-to-avatar translator for ASL, BSL and Libras, freemium. This comes from a listicle — [AI Tools Bakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/best-ai-sign-language-apps/)
- Signapse is a UK academic spin-out. Its SignStudio and SignStream products use generative photorealistic avatars to sign videos and live announcements in BSL and ASL, with transport customers — [Nimdzi](https://www.nimdzi.com/nimdzi-language-technology-radar-report-2025/), [sign.mt companies list](https://sign.mt/docs/docs/companies/sign-language-companies.html)
- SLAIT: the latest primary coverage is from 2021, describing an Aachen spin-out with a proof of concept for real-time ASL translation in video calls. I found no 2026 status — [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/26/slaits-real-time-sign-language-translation-promises-more-accessible-online-communication)
- Sign-Speak is a US, described as Deaf-owned, ASL recognition and captioning company. SignAll (Dolphio Technologies) does camera-based ASL-to-English. I found no 2026 news on either — [sign.mt list](https://sign.mt/docs/docs/companies/sign-language-companies.html), [Nimdzi](https://www.nimdzi.com/nimdzi-language-technology-radar-report-2025/)
- sign.mt is a Zurich company founded by Amit Moryossef, formerly of Sarah Ebling's UZH lab. Its demo translates text into 40+ sign languages, including Swiss German Sign Language. Per swissinfo, the output still has many errors, and its avatars are less expressive than humans and too slow for conversation. It currently uses statistical machine translation — [swissinfo, 13 Oct 2025](https://www.swissinfo.ch/ger/schweizer-ki/taugt-k%c3%bcnstliche-intelligenz-f%c3%bcr-geb%c3%a4rdensprache/90131119)

**Learning apps with real-time camera feedback**
- Nvidia Signs (with the American Society for Deaf Children and Hello Monday, early 2025) is a free web app with a 3D avatar teacher and real-time webcam feedback. It launched with 100 signs and covers hands only; facial expressions and head movements are not yet tracked. Its target is 400,000 clips for 1,000 words, crowdsourced and validated by fluent signers, with a public dataset promised — [Nvidia blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-sign-language), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/nvidia-has-built-a-free-ai-led-platform-to-help-teach-america-sign-language-with-400-000-video-clips-representing-1-000-signed-words-so-far)
- PopSign / PopSign AI (Georgia Tech, RIT/NTID, Google) is a bubble-shooter game for hearing parents of deaf children. PopSign AI uses the selfie camera and MediaPipe hand tracking with TFLite models to recognise the player's signs. A study found that practising sign production in PopSignAI teaches more efficiently than a receptive-only version. The PopSign v1.0 dataset is public — [RIT](https://www.rit.edu/ntid/news/parents-deaf-children-can-more-easily-learn-sign-language-thanks-powerful-tech-collaboration), [CNLSE paper record](https://cnlse.es/es/recursos/biblioteca/popsignai-towards-using-sign-language-recognition-games-to-improve-american-sign-language-learning-in-novice-signers), [dataset](https://signdata.cc.gatech.edu/view/datasets/popsign_v1_0/), [NeurIPS 2023 D&B](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/00dada608b8db212ea7d9d92b24c68de-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)
- Fingerspelling.xyz (Hello Monday and ASDC, 2021) is a browser app. The webcam checks hand alignment against letter shapes and gives feedback until the shape is correct, and webcam data is not stored. It is ASL only, and current maintenance status is unverified — [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2021/08/27/fingerspelling-xyz-app-learn-sign-language-alphabet-design/)
- SignScribe (iOS) offers camera recognition of the ASL alphabet. It handles static letters only (no J or Z) and is rated 3.4 from 15 reviews. TeachMeSign has a camera "Sign Check Mode" covering about 37 signs. AISA School claims instant camera corrections, with few details — [SignScribe](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6474526925), [TeachMeSign](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6736827510), [AISA](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6744299604)

**Swiss context**
- Switzerland has three sign languages (DSGS, LSF, LIS), used by about 30,000 people. SGB-FSS is the national umbrella body and the competence centre for technology and sign language — [EBGB/EDI guide](https://www.edi.admin.ch/dam/edi/de/dokumente/gleichstellung/E-Accessibility/leitfaden_gebaerdensprache.pdf.download.pdf/Leitfaden%20Geb%C3%A4rdensprache.pdf)
- Signwise, the SGB-FSS learning platform, has automatic feedback for about 100 signs (developed with UZH). SGB-FSS also offers a DSGS A1/A2 online placement test, and there is a Signwise app — [UZH IICT news](https://www.iict.uzh.ch/en/news/Einstufungstest-in-Signwise.html), [Signwise app](https://apps.apple.com/al/app/signwise/id1661099735). The search results did not make clear whether the automatic feedback covers LSF-CH.
- Other Swiss apps: ZGSZ is a DSGS vocabulary app, and Tess is a relay service connecting to sign or text interpreters, rated 3.2 from 37 reviews — [ZGSZ](https://apps.apple.com/cy/app/zgsz/id6447174943), [Tess](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/mymmx-tess-relay-dienste/id1056637235)
- SwissTXT (SRG) is building AI sign-language avatars, using 16-camera recordings of its 14 interpreters. A first live test was planned for early 2026 on weather and similar routine programmes — [swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/ger/schweizer-ki/taugt-k%c3%bcnstliche-intelligenz-f%c3%bcr-geb%c3%a4rdensprache/90131119)
- SGB-FSS spokesperson Ben Jud: the association follows the field "with interest but critically". Quote: "Derzeit ist uns kein Produkt bekannt, das unseren Erwartungen genügt" ("we currently know of no product that meets our expectations"). UZH's Sarah Ebling says digital interpreters can be justified where no human is available but are unacceptable in medical settings. The interpreters' association bgdü sees avatars as a possible supplement only — [swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/ger/schweizer-ki/taugt-k%c3%bcnstliche-intelligenz-f%c3%bcr-geb%c3%a4rdensprache/90131119)

**Deaf community attitudes**
- The SignAloud gloves (UW, 2016) drew an open letter from linguists and Deaf scholars. It said the gloves don't work as advertised, miss ASL grammar (use of signing space, facial expressions), put the whole burden on the Deaf person, were built without Deaf consultation, and promote audism — [Open letter PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/ebender/papers/SignAloudOpenLetter.pdf), [CACM 2018](https://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2018/12/232889-technology-for-the-deaf/fulltext)
- A 2026 Northeastern survey of Deaf and hard-of-hearing respondents: those with limited access to interpreters were more optimistic. Some support tools only if Deaf-led, and the technology should be "a tool, not a solution to replace real-life interpreters" — [Northeastern News, Mar 2026](https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/03/19/sign-language-technology-skepticism/)
- A 2026 arXiv paper reports strong skepticism among Deaf communities toward sign language translation. It notes that a major online Deaf community banned all posts proposing technology meant to "help" deaf people — [arXiv 2602.10235](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.10235)

### Inferences
- Avoid building "sign to text for Deaf people to talk to hearing people". Google SL2T now ships free on Pixel, and the burden-shifting critique applies directly. Judges with Tech-for-Good knowledge may mark it down.
- Better framing: hearing people learn, so the hearing side makes the effort. Examples: colleagues, classmates, parents, and SBB or front-desk staff learning 20 to 50 high-value signs in LSF-CH or DSGS through webcam feedback. This fits Scholé's "memorable teaching, not a chatbot", PopSign's evidence that production practice beats receptive-only practice, and the Logitech webcam challenge.
- Open technical angle: almost every learning tool checks hands only. Nvidia says so explicitly, and SignScribe handles static letters only. A tutor that also gives feedback on non-manual markers (eyebrows for questions, mouthing, head) or on movement and trajectory is a real gap. MediaPipe Holistic or FaceMesh blendshapes make it feasible in 20 hours.
- Swiss/LSF-CH angle: Signwise covers about 100 signs with feedback, apparently DSGS-focused. No product found offers LSF-CH camera feedback, and Lausanne is in the LSF area. Co-design with SGB-FSS or Deaf students (for example the Deaf communities at EPFL or UNIL) would answer the "Deaf-led" requirement.
- Frugal-AI angle (AXA): landmark-only processing (as SL2T does) on a laptop CPU, with no video leaving the device, is both frugal and privacy-preserving. It can be pitched with energy and latency numbers.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the camera-feedback features or current status of Lingvano, SignSchool, ASL Bloom, Hello Sign, or Google's "Fingerspelling with AI" experiment. No search returned primary sources.
- I could not confirm whether Nvidia released the Signs dataset publicly, or whether SignGemma open weights exist as of Nov 2026, or how SignGemma relates to SL2T.
- I found no 2026 status for SLAIT, SignAll or Sign-Speak.
- I could not confirm whether Signwise's automatic feedback covers LSF-CH, or whether any LSF-CH dataset is public.
- I could not confirm the results of the SwissTXT live avatar test planned for early 2026.
- I did not search Microsoft Research sign-language work or Devpost hackathon galleries, so I cannot say how saturated "ASL alphabet recognizer" projects are on Devpost. Anecdotally they are very common, but this is unsourced.

## Hands-free / motor-disability control

### Takeaway
Head and face cursor control from a webcam is a solved, free commodity. It is built into Apple platforms, Google Project Gameface is open source, and Enable Viacam is GPL. Talon covers voice plus eye control for power users. The remaining gaps are in the layers above the cursor: intent-level control (for example "join meeting", "mute", "next slide"), personalised and fatigue-aware gesture mapping, and helping people who are not experts set it up.

### Cited Findings
- Project Gameface (Google) controls the cursor with head movement and facial gestures. A desktop version came first, and the Android version was open-sourced in May 2024, using MediaPipe Face Landmarks and blendshape thresholds through the Android accessibility service. It tracks the forehead landmark by default for stability. I found no 2025–2026 updates — [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/project-gameface-launches-on-android/), [Google blog 2023](https://blog.google/technology/ai/google-project-gameface/)
- Apple: iOS/iPadOS 18 added Eye Tracking using the front camera, with dwell selection and calibration. Head Tracking under Switch Control needs a TrueDepth camera. macOS has Head Pointer, with clicks through switches, facial expressions or Voice Control — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/05/15/ios-18-accessibility-features), [Apple iPad guide](https://support.apple.com/en-lb/guide/ipad/eye-tracking-device-ipad2cd35723/16.0/ipados), [Apple Mac help](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/mac-help/mchlb2d4782b/11.0/mac/11.0)
- One user reports Apple's head tracking works better than its eye tracking, but not as well as external head-tracking hardware — [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/255987255)
- Hawkeye Access does gaze control with TrueDepth on iPad, iPhone, M1+ Mac and Vision Pro. Tobii TD Pilot is dedicated eye-tracking hardware for iPad — [Hawkeye](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/hawkeye-access/id1439231627), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2232491)
- Enable Viacam (eViacam) is head-tracking mouse replacement using a standard webcam with no extra hardware, licensed GPL-3 — [eviacam](https://eviacam.sourceforge.net)
- Talon Voice is proprietary freeware for voice control and coding. Its eye tracking is typically paired with a Tobii tracker, and it clicks with mouth "pops". Open alternatives are Caster/Aenea (Kaldi) — [talonvoice.com](https://talonvoice.com), [Hands-free coding notes](https://yduf.github.io/talon/), [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/computing/software/programming-by-voice-may-be-the-next-frontier-in-software-development)

### Inferences
- Rebuilding a head mouse in a hackathon is not original. A webcam-plus-microphone layer that maps a few reliable face gestures and short voice commands to high-level actions could be. Examples: launching an IT-fix flow (Nexthink), joining or muting a call, or answering an SBB rerouting prompt. Nexthink Spark's daily-agent framing fits this.
- Fatigue-aware adaptation is also open: detect from blendshape noise or dwell errors that the user is tiring, then enlarge targets or switch to voice. I found no product doing this, but my search was shallow, so treat that as a hypothesis.

### Gaps
- Windows Eye Control (it needs supported eye-tracker hardware) and Camera Mouse were not checked against current sources.
- I found no user studies on what motor-impaired users find missing in these tools.

## Live captions and meeting accessibility for deaf/hard-of-hearing participants

### Takeaway
Live captioning is everywhere: Live Transcribe and Live Caption, Teams, Zoom and Meet, Ava, Rogervoice. Research shows the remaining pain points are not raw captions. They are speaker identification, the loss of emotion and emphasis in captions, interpreter placement and clarity in video grids, and auto-caption errors (about 8% word errors in one test). I found no product or study offering "your name was mentioned" or "you were asked a question" alerts. That looks like an open, demo-friendly angle, but it is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Consumer Reports, with Northeastern and Pomona, found that auto-captions on BlueJeans, Webex, Meet and Zoom got roughly 8% of words wrong — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/disability-rights/auto-captions-often-fall-short-on-zoom-facebook-and-others-a9742392879)
- In a co-design study (McDonnell et al., CHI 2023) with mixed-hearing small groups, participants wanted basic captioning features such as speaker identification and customisation, plus behavioural feedback during conversation — [CHI 2023 PDF](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/media/publications/McDonnell_EasierOrHarderDependingOnWhoTheHearingPersonIsCodesigningVideoconferencingToolsForSmallGroupsWithMixedHearingStatus_CHI2023.pdf), [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10431246)
- A CHI study with eight Deaf and hard-of-hearing participants found that captions don't show emotion or emphasis, which limits inclusion in meetings with hearing peers. Sign interpreters also run into problems with placement in the video grid and the clarity of their signs — [doi 10.1145/3544548.3580809](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3580809)
- Small-group captioning involves social, environmental and technical factors, per McDonnell PACMHCI 2021 — [PDF](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/media/publications/McDonnell_SocialEnvironmentalAndTechnicalFactorsAtPlayInTheCurrentUseAndFutureDesignOfSmallGroupCaptioning_PACMHCI2021.pdf)
- A 2026 arXiv paper asks whether caption metrics are broken, looking at latency and how DHH users actually rate captions versus WER-style metrics — [arXiv 2609.11408](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11408)
- Live Transcribe & Sound Notifications (Android): real-time transcription, custom notifications, and detection of non-speech sounds such as alarms, clapping and music — [RNID](https://rnid.org.uk/information-and-support/technology-and-products/speech-to-text-smartphone-apps/)
- Ava: a DHH-focused captioning app. Free tier with unlimited basic captions and sessions up to 40 minutes; Premium claims about 5 errors per 100 words — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=me.ava.android)
- Rogervoice: captioned phone calls, 12M+ calls in 100+ languages, added free in-person captioning — [HearingTracker](https://hearingtracker.com/press-releases/rogervoice-brings-free-real-time-captioning-to-in-person-conversations)
- A W3C user story describes a Deaf student who relies on lip reading in unplanned calls without interpreters — [W3C WAI](https://w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/user-stories/story-six/)

### Inferences
- Open angles: (a) "attention cues". The local microphone and ASR detect your name, a direct question, or "any objections?", then a visual flash, a haptic signal (phone or watch) or a ring-light cue fires, along with a 10-second recap of context. (b) Captions that carry emphasis and emotion, for example marking prosody or the speaker's facial expression from the webcam. (c) Turn-taking cues from the camera, such as a warning that someone is about to speak, from lip movement or head turn. All three can be built locally with a Logitech webcam and mic, and they fit Nexthink DEX framed as inclusive meeting experience.
- Because research shows WER metrics don't match DHH experience (arXiv 2609.11408), a demo should measure latency and usefulness with real users, not just accuracy.

### Gaps
- I could not verify whether Teams, Zoom or Meet offer name-mention alerts for DHH users in 2026. Teams does have "@mention" chat notifications, but that is not speech-based and is unsourced here.
- I did not verify Otter.ai's 2026 accessibility features.

## Webcam "meeting wellness/productivity" tools and privacy criticism

### Takeaway
Logitech's own software (Logi Tune: RightSight auto-framing, image controls, Show Mode) focuses on framing and image quality, not wellness or learning. Consumer posture apps (Zen, WorkPose, Posture Monitor) exist, and they all lead with "processed locally" claims because of always-on-camera distrust. Employer-side webcam analytics are heavily criticised as "bossware". Nexthink Spark (launched Jan 2026) is a chat-style IT agent built on endpoint telemetry. I found no evidence that it uses webcam or microphone signals, so there is room for a sensing layer that the user controls.

### Cited Findings
- Logi Tune RightSight keeps the user centred in the frame. RightSight 2 handles facial expressions, gestures and body language and estimates people's distance from the camera on device. Tune also controls colour and brightness, resolution (720p to 4K) and Show Mode — [Logitech support](https://support.logi.com/hc/en-ca/articles/8375628302359-What-is-RightSight-and-where-can-I-find-it), [Logitech AI hub](https://hub.sync.logitech.com/learn/post/all-about-ai-on-logitech-devices-OX720pT8Zxtitym), [The Register on MX Brio 705](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2024/03/06/logitech-mx-brio-705-where-ultra-hd-meets-ultra-ai/286421)
- None of the Logitech results mention posture coaching, eye-contact correction or wellness reminders in Logi Tune — [search result set, Logitech support](https://support.logi.com/hc/en-au/articles/20713215679511-What-is-RightSight)
- Zen (2022) is a posture app with open-source posture code that runs locally, yet TechCrunch notes privacy-conscious workers may still dislike an always-on lens — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/29/zen-pre-seed-posture-correction/)
- WorkPose covers posture, eye strain, fatigue and focus, and says the camera feed is not uploaded. Posture Monitor (Mac) says processing is local with no images stored. Both claims are vendor self-reports — [Product Hunt WorkPose](https://www.producthunt.com/p/workpose/workpose), [Posture Monitor](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6751619063)
- CDT argues bossware can discourage and penalise health-enhancing behaviour such as taking breaks — [CDT report](https://cdt.org/?p=12710). The Register (Nov 2025) covers AI bossware judging performance — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/11/23/bossware_monitor_remote_employees/). The Irish Times (Aug 2026) is critical of workplace mood monitoring — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/work/2026/08/21/workplace-mood-monitoring-tech-may-not-be-something-to-smile-about/). Workers' resistance to surveillance is documented in a CHI-track paper — [arXiv 2412.06945](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06945v2)
- Nexthink Spark, launched 20 Jan 2026, is a "personal IT agent for every employee". It uses real-time endpoint telemetry, AI diagnostics and IT-approved automation to fix issues without tickets (for example VPN repair). Nexthink claims 77% first-contact resolution, a vendor figure — [Nexthink press](https://nexthink.com/press/nexthink-spark), [Nexthink blog](https://www.nexthink.com/blog/meet-spark), [ITmedia](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/enterprise/articles/2603/13/news008.html)

### Inferences
- The Nexthink and Logitech overlap is open: a "meeting-readiness" agent the employee opens before calls. It would check that the camera and mic actually work (frozen frames, a muted or wrong mic, backlighting, framing) and fix them through Tune-like controls or OS settings, all locally. Spark does device telemetry, and nothing found does perceptual AV checks before a meeting.
- Any wellness or focus feature must be designed against the bossware critique: on-device only, user-owned data, no manager dashboard, and nudges rather than scores. Naming this explicitly in the pitch will pre-empt judges' privacy objections.
- Accessibility-framed productivity (captions with attention cues, as in the previous section) is easier to defend than "focus tracking", which reads as surveillance.

### Gaps
- I did not verify Nvidia Broadcast's current features (Eye Contact, noise removal) or Microsoft Teams "intelligent" camera and wellbeing features as of 2026.
- I found no source on whether Nexthink offers collaboration or meeting-quality monitoring of webcams or mics, though its Collaboration Experience product may cover call quality. Unverified.

## Accessibility at train stations and SBB disruption data

### Takeaway
SBB Inclusive already pushes station and train info plus disruption announcements as text and audio, mainly for blind and partially sighted users, with deaf users as secondary beneficiaries. I found no sign-language content. Open data exists for building disruption tools: SIRI-SX/VDV736 event data and GTFS-RT Service Alerts on opentransportdata.swiss, both requiring an API key. The open gap is personalised, multimodal guidance during disruptions: sign-language or easy-language summaries, visual or haptic alerts tied to the user's own connection, and a station helper that the user can ask by camera or voice.

### Cited Findings
- SBB Inclusive gives visual, digital and audio customer information on the smartphone. When the app detects you are on an SBB long-distance train and a disruption occurs, it shows the announcements. It works at all Swiss railway stations and on SBB long-distance and regional trains, and some pages also list bus and tram stops. It targets blind and partially sighted users, and SBB also says it helps deaf and hard-of-hearing travellers — [SBB Inclusive](https://www.sbb.ch/en/travel-information/apps/sbb-inclusive.html), [SBB travel planning](https://www.sbb.ch/en/travel-information/individual-needs/travelling-reduced-mobility/travel-planning.html), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sbb-inclusive/id1495023290)
- SBB also offers easy-language and easy-to-read information pages — [SBB easy language](https://www.sbb.ch/en/travel-information/individual-needs/travelling-reduced-mobility/easy-language.html)
- ZVV: hearing-impaired passengers can use a Procom SMS service for disruptions, and the ZVV app sends route disruption alerts — [ZVV](https://www.zvv.ch/en/service/hindernisfrei-reisen/reisen-mit-hoerbehinderung.html)
- SIRI-SX/VDV736 event data covers all unplanned incident information from transport companies connected to the central hub (SKI DDS). The endpoint is api.opentransportdata.swiss/la/siri-sx (XML) and needs an API key. Recommended pattern: one full initial load, then updates every few hours. The Swiss realization guide v1.0 is dated 1 July 2024, and the format has been used in Switzerland since April 2022 — [opendata.swiss SIRI-SX](https://opendata.swiss/en/dataset/ereignisinformation-ov-schweiz-siri-sx-vdv736), [Realization guide PDF](https://www.tp-info.ch/sites/default/files/2024-07/realization_guide_siri-sx_oev_schweiz_v1.0.pdf)
- A GTFS-Realtime Service Alerts feed is available as an alternative, also keyed — [GTFS-RT SA](https://ckan.opendata.swiss/dataset/gtfs-realtime-service-alerts1)
- Signapse already sells AI sign-language avatars for live transport announcements (BSL/ASL, UK transport customers), so "announcements in sign language" has a commercial precedent abroad — [sign.mt list](https://sign.mt/docs/docs/companies/sign-language-companies.html), [Nimdzi](https://www.nimdzi.com/nimdzi-language-technology-radar-report-2025/)

### Inferences
- An original SBB angle: a "disruption companion". It takes a SIRI-SX or GTFS-RT alert for the traveller's own trip, shows a plain-language or easy-language reroute (an LLM summary plus the next viable connection), and cues it visually or by haptic, so it works for deaf users. It could also show a small set of pre-recorded DSGS/LSF clips for standard disruption phrases, made with Deaf signers rather than generated avatars. That avoids both the avatar-quality criticism and the cost of a Signapse-style avatar.
